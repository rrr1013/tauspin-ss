## Ranked findings

1. **High — The central \(m=2\rightarrow m=0\) transfer is plausible, not established.** The decomposition in [transfer_a1.py](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/transfer_a1.py:3) is correct in the ultra-relativistic limit. Exact SM Drell–Yan formulas show \(C_{nn}+C_{rr}=0\) only for \(\beta_\tau=1\); finite mass generates a transverse trace at \(O(1-\beta_\tau^2)\) and longitudinal–transverse terms at \(O(m_\tau/q)\), including \(\gamma^*/Z\) interference. At the pole, \(1-\beta_\tau^2=4m_\tau^2/M_Z^2\simeq1.5\times10^{-3}\), so the \(m=0\) contamination is negligible, but “only \(m=2\)” is not exact. [Exact SM density matrix](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-023-11307-2). High-\(q_T\) longitudinal/tensor Z states rotate and reshape \(m=2\), but do not create \(m=0\) when \(m_\tau=0\). Equality of responses requires more than per-tau factorisation: conformal single-tau response, zero odd angular bias, independence from truth/selection, and no shared event error. Shared PV, MET/recoil, joint-network inference, charge biases, or a common basis rotation break it—indeed a common rotation cancels in \(m=0\) but dilutes \(m=2\). **Fix:** qualify C1 and test a full correlated response matrix, not just independent complex gains.

2. **High — \(r=\mathrm{Cov}_{\rm flat}(O,S)\) is a valid local derivative, but not itself a data/MC calibration observable.** It equals \(dE[O]/dc|_{c=0}\), provided the reweighted mixture really has a common spin-flat base density. The weight in [transfer_a1.py](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/transfer_a1.py:54) additionally assumes H and Z differ only through the stated spin factors after parent-overlap weighting. Real data cannot evaluate \(S(h_{\rm true})\); it measures a fitted Z-template coefficient, conditional on correct unpolarised shape, Z tensor, backgrounds, kinematics and selection. The double ratio is correct only for a diagonal multiplicative response. Here \(Q\) is merely the equal average of \(Q_1,Q_2\) ([transfer_a1.py](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/transfer_a1.py:70)), while the actual Z weights their event-dependent combination; cross-responses are already 4.9% and 3.7% overall ([transfer_a1.json](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_a1.json:2014)). **Fix:** implement a joint Z likelihood using \(C_Z^{ij}O_{ij}\), extract the complete \(2\times2\) transverse response/mixing matrix, and propagate it into the H likelihood.

3. **High — C6 does not substantiate detector absorption.** The largest local-network test is only \(r_T=0.9955\) for a \(-5\%\) \(\pi^0\) scale, with residual 1.0009 ([transfer_calib.json](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_calib.json:3)); the no-IP/SV network gives 0.9684 and residual 1.0111 ([same JSON](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_calib.json:2174)). These are scale variations, not serious transverse-resolution tests. Most dangerous are shared-PV position/refit errors, IP covariance/tails and alignment, \(\pi^0\) angular resolution and mode migration, MET/recoil resolution, and mismodelling of the reconstructed tau/CS frames. The C5 frame alone worsens \(14.91\rightarrow16.20\) in \(\sigma_\kappa\sqrt N\) ([z_state.json](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/z_state.json:33)). The cited producer `analysis/calibration_response/evaluate_remote.py` is absent, so C6 is not reproducible from this checkout. **Fix:** correlated nuisance ensembles with PV/MET/frame/resolution variations, followed by the actual Z fit and H closure.

4. **Medium — C5 is an order-of-magnitude model, not yet a fair precision forecast.** The sign and factor \(-2\sqrt{1-A_\tau^2}\) in [z_state.py](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/z_state.py:69) reproduce \(C_{nn}=-C_{rr}=+0.989\sin^2\theta/(1+\cos^2\theta)\) in its conventions. But it is correct only for the restricted parity-even tensor \(N\). Real Z production has \(A_{0\ldots7}\); ATLAS observes \(A_0-A_2\neq0\) and nonzero \(A_{5,6,7}\), while vector terms also change the denominator. [ATLAS angular coefficients](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.00689). The moment variance is evaluated in the flat world, not at SM \(\kappa=1\), and omits backgrounds/nuisances. \(N_Z=1.09\)M is arithmetically plausible: 46,065 Run-2 Z events ([ent_hllhc.py](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/ip_tau_reco/ent_hllhc.py:24)) scaled by \(3000/139\times1.15\times0.9541\), then 0.718 with both polarimeters. Using H(125) kinematics for Z(91) materially biases boost, frame and resolution—probably tens of percent in the 16.2 constant, though not enough to overturn “statistics are ample.” **Fix:** genuine Z(91) full-ME sample, full \(A_i\)/\(\gamma^*\) state, category matching and a likelihood forecast.

5. **Medium — C4 is implemented mostly correctly, but “free” is a degeneracy and the conclusion is narrow.** The Gaussian prior is \(k_T=1+\sigma_T\theta\) with \(\theta\sim N(0,1)\); “free” is unpenalised and unbounded ([ent_robust.py](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/ip_tau_reco/ent_robust.py:199)). Thus \(k_T=30.35\), and 1233 with \(\tau=1\), are arbitrary finite points on the \(k_T\to\infty,\ c_T\to0\) degeneracy, not estimates. `BoundModel` correctly imposes \(0<k<k_{\max}\) via a sigmoid; it is a hard bound, not a “flat-ish” prior. The Cauchy–Schwarz derivation is correct if MC bin probabilities and \(E[p_i^2]\) apply to data. Numerically C4 is reproduced: \(1.846\rightarrow1.837\) at 30%, \(1.821\) at 50%, bound \(0.967\); with auxiliary control \(3.025\rightarrow2.982\rightarrow2.903\) ([kt_requirement.json](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/kt_requirement.json:78)). **Conclusion:** scalar \(k_T\) statistical precision is not the bottleneck; response nonfactorisation and background-shape validity may be.

6. **Medium — C2 is a useful targeted success, not proof that current uncertainty is absorbed.** For any-3π, the learned network moves by 0.9375 while the double ratio is 0.9977 [0.979,1.015]; for 3π×3π, 0.8631 and 0.978 [0.907,1.056] ([transfer_a1.json](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_a1.json:208), [same](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_a1.json:805)). This supports CLEO-vs-generator transfer, but the latter interval permits 9% nonclosure and CP-odd \(A/Q=1.07\). It is one importance-reweighted current/lineshape benchmark, not an uncertainty envelope. **Fix:** several current eigenvariations/lineshapes plus reconstruction-efficiency and decay-mode-migration effects.

7. **Medium — C3 argues for stratification, not universal transfer.** Overall \(T/Q=1.0006\), but \(\pi\times3\pi=0.9580\) and \(3\pi\times3\pi=1.0790\); the softest-energy quartile is 1.0298 ([transfer_kin.json](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_kin.json:394), [same](/Users/ryunosuke/Projects/tauspin-wt-zcalib/analysis/z_calibration/results/transfer_kin.json:590)). **Fix:** calibrate by decay-mode pair and kinematics, with shrinkage rather than one global factor.

8. **Low — Novelty supports a paper section, not a standalone paper.** Berge–Bernreuther–Kirchner already proposed DY subsets for calibration of \(\phi^*_{CP}\) ([arXiv:1408.0798](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.0798)); ATLAS has used Z→ττ control distributions; Zhang et al. reconstruct Z-pole tomography with fixed analysing powers ([arXiv:2504.01496](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01496v2)); TauPolaris already claims learned-polarimeter H entanglement/CP gains ([arXiv:2608.10961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10961)). The potentially new element is the representation-aware \(m=2\rightarrow m=0\) transfer/nonclosure study for a learned polarimeter and hadronic-current uncertainty.

**Overall verdict:** **revise / approve only as a methodological section.** The physics intuition and C2/C4 numerical diagnostics are credible, and Z statistics are clearly sufficient. The proposed verdict overstates what has been demonstrated: no realistic Z fit yet measures the same response needed by H, and the correlated PV/frame/resolution effects most capable of separating \(m=2\) from \(m=0\) remain untested. The strongest next direction is a joint Z+H likelihood with a full transverse response matrix, category/mode dependence, full-ME Z(91) events, and correlated detector nuisances; after that, prioritize background-shape control, since the scalar calibration precision is already not limiting.
